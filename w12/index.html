Week 12 · Practical P12 · 2 hours

Role-play consultation: bus lane and parking reform on Shahrisabz St

Objective

Conduct a role-play consultation on a bus lane and parking reform, then revise the proposal in response to the discussion, distinguishing evidence, interests and values (LO5, LO7).

Tasks

Task 1. The proposal on the table

The city proposes, for Shahrisabz St between its two ends: (1) kerbside bus lanes in both directions from 07:00 to 20:00; (2) paid short-stay parking on side streets near shops and the market; (3) loading bays reserved 08:00–11:00; (4) a median refuge and longer pedestrian green at the main junction with Istiqbol St. Use your LAB10 and LAB11 results as the evidence base. One student chairs (city project manager); one records every comment on the LAB12 template.

Deliverable: Proposal summary on one slide or flipchart

Task 2. Prepare your role (10 minutes)

Each student receives one role card from the table below. Read your position, interests and red line. Prepare three arguments, one question for the city and one condition under which you could accept the proposal. Stay in role; do not invent facts — use only the evidence in the room.

Deliverable: Three arguments, one question and one condition, written on your card

Task 3. Run the consultation (40 minutes)

Round 1 (15 min): the chair presents; each role states its position in one minute. Round 2 (15 min): open discussion; the chair must ask every role at least once and steer from positions to interests. Round 3 (10 min): the chair proposes changes; roles say whether their condition is met. The recorder enters every comment (role group, theme, stance, short text — no names) in the LAB12 template Feedback sheet.

Deliverable: Completed Feedback sheet from the role-play

Task 4. Classify what was said

After the role-play, as a group, label each comment as evidence (a testable claim), interest, or value. Use the LAB12 template Response_register to group comments by theme and count support, conditional and opposition.

Deliverable: Classified comment list and theme counts

Task 5. Revise the proposal

Write a revised proposal (one page): what you accept, modify, explain or reject for each theme, and why. State which change costs the bus passengers something and whether it is worth it. Fill the response columns of the register.

Deliverable: Revised proposal and completed response register — input to course-project component 5 (policy and governance)

Materials

  • Role cards (table in Task 2), cut out and handed out
  • LAB12_register_template.xlsx on one laptop
  • LAB10 and LAB11 results as evidence
  • Flipchart or projector

Assessment

Continuous assessment (LO5, LO7): quality of in-role arguments and listening; correct classification of evidence, interests and values; a revised proposal that answers the main objections and states its trade-offs.

Week 12 · Laboratory LAB12 · 2 hours · QGIS, Excel (QGIS 3.40 LTR or newer)

Stakeholder matrix and consultation response register

Objective

Prepare a stakeholder matrix with interest and influence scoring, analyse an anonymised feedback table, group objections by theme and stakeholder, check who took part, and develop a project-response register (LO5, LO7).

Data

SYNTHETIC — generated for teaching: LAB12_synthetic_consultation.xlsx. These files imitate realistic Tashkent conditions but are not observations; the README sheet records the method and random seed.

FileDescriptionSource
LAB12_corridor.gpkgLayers: corridor_street (Shahrisabz St), consultation_area (300 m), institutions (education, health, community, commercial; OSM), bus_stops, traffic_signals, roads_context© OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL)
LAB12_synthetic_consultation.xlsxSYNTHETIC consultation for the bus-lane and parking reform: Stakeholders (illustrative 1–5 scores, live interest, influence, quadrant and method formulas), Thresholds, Methods, Feedback (160 anonymised comments), Objection_matrix, Response_register (with chart), RepresentationSynthetic teaching data
LAB12_register_template.xlsxBlank template with the same formulas for your own corridor or the P12 role-play: Stakeholders, Feedback (300 rows), Response_registerSUMP Guidelines (2019)

All GIS layers use EPSG:32642 (WGS 84 / UTM zone 42N). Set the QGIS project CRS to EPSG:32642 before starting.

Steps

Step 1. Who is affected? (QGIS)

  1. Project > Properties > CRS: set EPSG:32642; add all layers of LAB12_corridor.gpkg
  2. Symbology of institutions: Categorized by category
  3. Vector analysis > Statistics by categories: input institutions, category field = category (all institutions lie inside the consultation area)
  4. Right-click bus_stops > Show Feature Count; check with Vector analysis > Count points in polygon (polygon = consultation_area)

Check: 18 education, 31 health, 8 community and 322 commercial places; 30 bus stops within 300 m

Step 2. Score the stakeholders

  1. Open LAB12_synthetic_consultation.xlsx; read the README (SYNTHETIC comments, illustrative scores, seed)
  2. On Stakeholders, check the formulas in columns I–M (Formulas > Show Formulas)
  3. Read the quadrant counts below the table

Check: 18 stakeholders: 4 manage closely, 2 keep satisfied, 8 keep informed, 4 monitor; 3 flagged for targeted outreach; highest influence = City khokimiyat, transport department

Step 3. Re-score and test the thresholds

  1. As a group, change the scores of three stakeholders you disagree with; record why in a new column N
  2. On Thresholds, change the influence cut-off from 3 to 2.5 and see which stakeholders move; restore 3
  3. Insert > Chart > Scatter: interest (column I) against influence (column J)

Check: A scatter chart and a short note on which quadrant assignments are sensitive to the cut-off

Step 4. Group the objections

  1. On Feedback, use Data > Filter to read the comments for one theme
  2. On Objection_matrix, read objections by theme and respondent group
  3. On Response_register, read counts by theme and the group with most objections

Check: 160 comments; 40% oppose; theme with most objections = Traffic congestion (18), mostly from car commuters

Step 5. Check who took part

  1. On Representation, read shares by gender, age band, channel and respondent group
  2. Find reference shares for the corridor population (district statistics or your own count at bus stops) and enter them in column D
  3. Write two sentences on which groups are under-represented and what you would do about it

Check: Women 27.5% of comments, aged 60+ 11.9%, public meetings 33.1%, bus passengers 11.9%

Step 6. Build the response register

  1. On Response_register, choose a response type for each theme from the drop-down list (Accept / Modify / Explain / Reject)
  2. Write the project change or the explanation, an owner and a target date
  3. For the top objection theme, link your response to the LAB10 junction evidence

Check: Every theme has a response type, a change or explanation, and an owner

Step 7. Set up the template for your corridor

  1. Open LAB12_register_template.xlsx; replace the theme names on Response_register with your own
  2. Enter the P12 role-play comments (or your field comments) on Feedback — no names or contact details

Check: The template's counts update as you type; on Response_register, All themes (B13) equals Rows entered on Feedback (B14), so B15 = 0

Submission checklist

  • Map of affected institutions within 300 m (with legend, source and date)
  • Stakeholder matrix with your re-scores and the scatter chart
  • Objection summary: top three themes and the groups behind them
  • Representation check with reference shares and two sentences on under-represented groups
  • Completed response register (all themes) and half a page of limitations: synthetic comments, illustrative scores, self-selection
  • Input data, method, results and limitations are all included (syllabus §6).
