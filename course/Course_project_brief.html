Course project · LO1–LO8 · Weeks 1–15

Integrated course project: multimodal improvement of a Tashkent corridor

The task

Develop an integrated project for a corridor, street or interchange agreed with the instructor. Compare the baseline, a low-cost improvement alternative and a structural-change alternative using a common set of appraisal criteria. Where forecasts are used, the baseline scenario must refer to the same future assessment year.

You work in a team on one corridor through the whole semester. Every practical (P1–P15) applies that week's method to your corridor, and every laboratory (LAB1–LAB15) teaches the tool on Tashkent-wide or example data that you then repeat for your corridor. The project is therefore built week by week; the five stages below are the points at which the instructor checks it.

Project preparation is integrated into independent study and scheduled classes. No additional contact hours or total workload are introduced (syllabus §5).

Required components (syllabus §5)

  1. Problem and objectives: study boundary, user groups, key constraints and 5–7 outcome indicators.
  2. Baseline analysis: network, street cross-section, vehicle and passenger flows, stops, walking and cycling connections, parking and data quality.
  3. Design alternatives: network and street space, public transport service, at least one junction or crossing, and a parking or loading solution.
  4. Calculations: demand scenario, person throughput or service capacity, delay, accessibility, preliminary costs and an environmental indicator. Explain methods and assumptions.
  5. Policy and governance: distribution of benefits and costs, stakeholders, matters requiring agreement and implementation risks.
  6. Selection of an alternative: multi-criteria appraisal, sensitivity analysis for at least one important assumption, a preferred alternative and its limitations.
  7. Implementation and monitoring: phases, responsible actors, capital and operating requirements, and arrangements for measuring outcomes.

Deliverables

  • An A4 report of at least 20 pages.
  • Principal plans and cross-sections in A3 format.
  • Calculation files (Excel with live formulas) and a GIS project (QGIS, EPSG:32642).
  • A register of data sources (use the LAB1 dataset log and the LAB15 Data_register template).
  • A 1–2-page final policy brief.
  • A presentation for the project defence.
  • All materials organised in one project folder. Individual contributions are assessed during the defence.

Stages and deadlines

Each stage is submitted to the instructor at the end of the deadline week and returned with comments on the five-point scale; later stages may revise earlier ones. The final mark is given on the complete report and defence (Marking_guides.docx).

Stage 1 checklist — due Week 3: problem, objectives and baseline data

  • Corridor boundary, assessment year and a two-line justification (P1 task 1).
  • Problem tree with evidence status marked, user-group table, 3–5 objectives (P1).
  • Indicator table: 5–7 outcome indicators, each with unit, data source and direction of improvement (P1, tested in P1 task 6).
  • A3 corridor map with stops, metro stations and key destinations, and the dataset log with source and date of every layer (LAB1).
  • Field-survey plan, recording form and pilot counts; vehicle flows versus person flows (P2).
  • Data-quality log for your own survey data (LAB2).
  • Walking catchments on the pedestrian network for the stops or stations nearest your corridor, compared with straight-line buffers (LAB3).
  • Justice statement: who benefits and who bears the costs, by user group (P3).

Stage 2 checklist — due Week 6: network and service alternatives

  • Map of existing network layers (walk, cycle, public transport, car, freight) with missing links and barriers (P4, LAB4).
  • Low-cost improvement alternative and structural-change alternative, mapped and listed with trade-offs (P4).
  • Network density and circuity for your corridor area and a ranked list of candidate missing connections (LAB4).
  • Scenario definition table, assumption register and horizon-year person demand and mode shares for the baseline and both alternatives (P5).
  • Sensitivity test of the weakest demand assumption (LAB5 tornado method applied to your corridor).
  • Bus service calculation for your corridor: frequency, headway, cycle time, fleet and offered capacity in passengers per hour; selected service alternative with justification (P6).
  • Effect of one priority measure on cycle time, fleet and waiting time (LAB6).

Stage 3 checklist — due Week 10: street design and operational calculations

  • Existing cross-section and two alternatives within the same width, dimensioned, with a stop-access layout (P7).
  • Space allocation and hourly person throughput by mode for the three sections (LAB7).
  • Redesigned pedestrian crossing and cycle facility through one junction and one stop (P8).
  • Barrier map and the three most serious safety deficiencies on your corridor (LAB8).
  • Asset inventory and maintenance and adaptation priorities (shade, drainage) for your corridor (P9, LAB9).
  • Junction calculations for the baseline and two control alternatives: capacity, degree of saturation, queues and delay, with vehicle, passenger and pedestrian delay reported separately (P10, LAB10).
  • Outline of the parking or loading solution (developed fully in Week 11).

Stage 4 checklist — due Week 14: integrated appraisal and implementation plan

  • Parking and loading policy with occupancy, turnover and loading-bay sizing (P11, LAB11).
  • Stakeholder matrix, consultation response register and the revised proposal — component 5 (P12, LAB12).
  • Decarbonisation package and one environmental indicator with a low / central / high range (P13, LAB13).
  • Appraisal matrix for the baseline and two alternatives on common criteria, with criterion weights, cost ranges and at least one sensitivity test; check for double counting (P14, LAB14).
  • Preferred alternative and its limitations; phased implementation plan with actors, capital and operating requirements, risks and indicators (P14).

Stage 5 checklist — due Week 15: final presentation and defence

  • Complete project folder submitted 24 hours before the defence slot, with the completed LAB15 reproducibility checklist.
  • Monitoring plan: corridor indicator table with baseline, target, frequency, responsible organisation and source; before–after design with a comparison site (LAB15).
  • Data register covering every dataset in the project (LAB15 Data_register).
  • Report (A4, at least 20 pages), A3 plans and cross-sections, calculation files, GIS project, 1–2-page policy brief.
  • Defence presentation and answers to the panel on technical design, the policy package and the monitoring plan (P15); individual contribution statement.

Rules

  • The corridor, street or interchange must be agreed with the instructor by the end of Week 1.
  • All three options (baseline, low-cost, structural) are assessed on the same criteria and, where forecasts are used, for the same future assessment year.
  • Label every number as measured, sourced or assumed. Synthetic lab data are for practice only and must not be presented as observations of your corridor.
  • Fieldwork is done from safe observation locations. Do not collect personally identifying data; report survey results in aggregate; blur faces and number plates in photos.
  • All GIS layers in EPSG:32642; every map with legend, scale, north arrow, source and date.
  • Calculation files keep live formulas so that the panel can trace each key number to its inputs.

Assessment

The course project is assessed on the report, calculation files, maps and an individual defence against LO1–LO8 (syllabus §6), using the course-project report rubric and the defence rubric in Marking_guides.docx and the five-point scale. Grades are combined in accordance with the university's approved procedures; this brief introduces no new percentage weights or pass thresholds.
