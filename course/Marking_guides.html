Marking guides

How to use the rubrics

These rubrics turn the five-point grading criteria of the syllabus into descriptors for each kind of work. A grade is given for the work as a whole: read across the rows, then choose the column that best describes the submission. The rubrics introduce no percentage weights or pass thresholds; grades are combined in accordance with the university's approved procedures.

Laboratory submissions must include input data, methods, results and limitations, rather than only completed maps or model outputs (syllabus §6). A submission missing one of the four is incomplete and cannot receive more than 3 until corrected.

Five-point grading criteria (syllabus §6)

GradeRequired performance
5 — ExcellentIndependently analyses the problem; provides consistent, verifiable calculations; compares alternatives against technical, social and environmental criteria; tests uncertainty; and defends a feasible recommendation.
4 — GoodProvides correct principal calculations and maps, justified alternatives and policy-impact analysis. Some limitations or sensitivity issues are not fully explored.
3 — SatisfactoryApplies basic concepts and simple calculations and explains an acceptable alternative. Analysis of alternatives, equity or implementation is limited; significant errors can be corrected.
2 — UnsatisfactoryCannot apply essential concepts; calculations or sources contain major errors; the solution lacks evidential support, or mandatory work is incomplete.
