Week 3 · Practical P3 · 2 hours

Who benefits and who pays? User problems and incidence on your corridor

Objective

Compare the problems experienced by different users of your corridor and identify who would benefit from the corridor project and who would bear its costs (LO5).

Tasks

Task 1. Compare problems across user groups

Take the user-group table from P1. For each group, describe the main problem on the corridor in one sentence and the evidence for it (your survey, LAB3 catchments, the police register summary, Pulatova's or Ergashova's findings). Mark where no evidence exists for a group.

Deliverable: Table: user group × problem × evidence × gap

Task 2. Walking access for slower walkers

In LAB3 the catchment uses 1,200 m (15 minutes at about 4.8 km/h). Repeat the network catchment for the station nearest your corridor with a shorter distance representing a slower walker (state your assumed speed and its source) and compare the areas. Explain who loses access.

Deliverable: Two catchment maps and a sentence on the difference

Task 3. Benefit–cost incidence

For the improvement you are considering, fill the incidence table from LEC6 for at least five groups, including at least one non-user group. State which justice principle (sufficiency, worst-off-first, egalitarian, utilitarian) you use to judge the result.

Deliverable: Completed incidence table and a statement of the principle

GroupBenefitsCostsSize of effect (high/medium/low)Evidence

Task 4. Critical reading: a claimed benefit

Open the Critical_reading sheet of LAB03_catchments.xlsx. Reproduce Pulatova's E = 365 × N × R and her $19,007,010 per year. List every input and mark it as observation, assumption or borrowed value. Add a distance term, trips per day and car occupancy, and change the share of residents who previously drove. Write five lines on what the figure can and cannot support.

Deliverable: Recalculated values with labelled inputs and five lines of critique

Task 5. Justice statement for Stage 1

Write half a page: the worst-off users on your corridor, how your indicators will be split by group, and which costs your project would impose on whom.

Deliverable: Justice statement, included in Stage 1

Materials

  • P1 user-group table
  • LAB3 maps and workbook (Critical_reading sheet)
  • LEC6 incidence template

Assessment

Continuous assessment (LO5): evidence-based comparison of user problems, a complete incidence table with a stated principle, and a correct, well-labelled recalculation of the benefit claim.

Week 3 · Laboratory LAB3 · 2 hours · QGIS, Excel (QGIS 3.40 LTR or newer)

Walking catchments on the network versus straight-line buffers

Objective

Calculate access catchments for metro stations and other origins on the pedestrian network, compare them with straight-line buffers using Pulatova's index, and measure how much residential land each district has within walking reach (LO2, LO5).

Data

FileDescriptionSource
LAB03_catchments.gpkgLayers: districts, walk_network (OSM roads except motorways + footways, largest connected part only), metro_stations, pulatova_origins (12 district centroids + Amir Temur Square), metro_circles_1200m, metro_network_catchments_1200m, pulatova_origin_catchments_1200m (reference results), residential_land (OSM), pop_grid (Kontur), example_corridor (Abdulla Qodiriy street ± 400 m, as in LAB1), corridor_bus_stops, corridor_schools, corridor_stop_catchments_500m and corridor_school_catchments_800m (reference results)© OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL); Kontur Population (2023); Pulatova (2024)
LAB03_catchments.xlsxSheets: Summary (formulas), Stations (index and rating formulas), Pulatova_compare (ranks and rank correlation), Districts (coverage shares), Critical_reading (Pulatova's benefit formula with editable assumptions), Stops_services (bus stops and schools in the example corridor: areas, index formulas, coverage of residential land)© OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL); Pulatova (2024)

All GIS layers use EPSG:32642 (WGS 84 / UTM zone 42N). Set the QGIS project CRS to EPSG:32642 before starting.

Steps

Step 1. Set up the project

  1. Project > Properties > CRS: EPSG:32642; add all layers of LAB03_catchments.gpkg
  2. Hide the reference result layers (circles and all catchment layers) until you have built your own

Check: 49 metro stations and 13 Pulatova origins; walk_network has 71,697 features

Step 2. Straight-line catchment for one station

  1. Select the station Chilonzor (Select Features by Value: name = Chilonzor)
  2. Vector geometry > Buffer: distance 1200, segments 64, Selected features only
  3. Field Calculator: area = $area

Check: Area ≈ 4,523,893 m² (π × 1,200²)

Step 3. Network catchment for the same station

  1. Processing Toolbox > Network analysis > Service area (from layer): vector layer representing network = walk_network; start points = metro_stations (Selected features only); path type = Shortest; travel cost = 1200; topology tolerance = 0
  2. Vector geometry > Buffer the output lines: distance 50, Dissolve result = yes
  3. Vector overlay > Clip: input = buffered lines, overlay = the 1,200 m circle from step 2
  4. Field Calculator: area = $area; index = 100 * area / 4523893

Check: Compare with row Chilonzor in the Stations sheet (column E); your area should agree within 3%

Step 4. Repeat for all stations in Excel

  1. Open LAB03_catchments.xlsx, sheet Stations: check the circle formula (F), the index (G) and the rating (H)
  2. Sort by index; note the five lowest stations and look at each on the map

Check: Mean index 56.7; lowest Yangiobod 14.0; highest Xalqlar doʻstligi 73.2; no station rated good (≥ 80)

Step 5. Compare network and circle coverage of residential land

  1. Show metro_network_catchments_1200m and metro_circles_1200m. Vector geometry > Dissolve each layer (no field) to get two union polygons
  2. Vector overlay > Intersection: residential_land with each union; Field Calculator $area; Statistics by categories by district (after Join attributes by location with districts)
  3. Compare with the Districts sheet columns E and F; read the shares in G and H

Check: Union areas 84.72 km² (network) vs 131.65 km² (circles); residential land within reach 23.8% vs 33.7%; Chilonzor 40.5%, Uchtepa 0.5%

Step 6. Replicate Pulatova's thirteen origins

  1. Run Service area (from layer) with start points = pulatova_origins, then buffer, dissolve and clip as in step 3
  2. Sheet Pulatova_compare: compare your index with hers (column E); read the rank correlation at the bottom
  3. Explain the largest difference (Yakkasaroy) in three sentences: centroid, map date, area method

Check: Rank correlation 0.76; Yakkasaroy 47.2 vs 76

Step 7. Bus stops and services on a corridor

  1. Show example_corridor, corridor_bus_stops and corridor_schools. Run Service area (from layer) with start points = corridor_bus_stops and travel cost = 500 (the SNiP 2.07.01-89 limit for the walk to a stop, quoted by Pulatova 2024, p.30); then buffer 50 m, dissolve and clip each catchment to its 500 m circle as in step 3
  2. Repeat for corridor_schools with travel cost = 800 (the 10-minute band)
  3. Sheet Stops_services: check the circle formula (F = PI()*D^2) and the index (G); read the means and the residential-land coverage at the bottom (Vector overlay > Intersection of residential_land with example_corridor and with the dissolved stop catchments, then $area)
  4. Repeat both runs for the stops and one service type (schools, clinics or markets) on your own corridor

Check: Example corridor: 49 bus stops, mean index 63.9 at 500 m; 16 schools, mean index 44.5 at 800 m; residential land within 500 m of a stop 84.2% on the network vs 94.2% in a straight line

Step 8. Population — a warning step

  1. Read the Kontur rows in the Summary sheet. Compare with the district populations in the LAB1 note
  2. Write one paragraph on why the course uses residential land instead of population for this lab

Check: Kontur gives 403,300 residents in the network union and 647,600 in the circle union — figures that cannot be trusted for Tashkent

Submission checklist

  • Map of the network and straight-line catchments for the three stations nearest your corridor (legend, scale, source and date)
  • Map and table of 500 m bus-stop catchments and one service type on your corridor, network versus straight line
  • Completed Stations and Districts sheets with your QGIS areas for those stations in an extra column
  • Paragraph comparing your replication of Pulatova's index with her published values
  • Limitations: OSM completeness, the 50 m strip, one walking distance for everyone, no crossing delays or slopes
  • Input data, method, results and limitations are all included (syllabus §6).
