Week 6 · Practical P6 · 2 hours

Headways, departures and fleet for your corridor's bus service

Objective

Calculate service headways, departures and fleet requirements from the passenger demand of your corridor, compare service alternatives and select one for course-project Stage 2 (LO4).

Tasks

Task 1. Collect the route inputs

For the bus route(s) on your corridor, take the route length and number of stops from LAB06_bus_routes.gpkg (layer bus_routes). Take the horizon-year peak-hour passengers at the busiest point from your P5 results (or from your Stage 1 counts grown to the horizon year). State the running speed and time per stop you assume and where they come from (an on-board stopwatch survey is best).

Deliverable: Input table with status (MEASURED / SOURCED / ASSUMPTION) for every value

Task 2. Frequency, headway and departures

Compute the demand frequency f = peak load ÷ (vehicle capacity × load factor) and compare it with a policy minimum (for example 4 buses per hour). Use the higher value. Headway h = 60 ÷ f. Round to a timetable-friendly headway and list the departures from 07:00 to 09:00.

Deliverable: Frequency, headway and the departure list for the morning peak

QuantityFormulaYour value
Demand frequency (buses/h)peak load ÷ (capacity × load factor)
Policy frequency (buses/h)60 ÷ policy headway
Design frequencyhigher of the two
Headway (min)60 ÷ design frequency
Departures 07:00–09:002 × design frequency

Task 3. Cycle time and fleet

Running time = length ÷ speed × 60 + stops × time per stop ÷ 60 (minutes). Cycle time C = 2 × running time × (1 + layover share). Fleet N = C ÷ h rounded up; operated headway = C ÷ N. Worked example (LAB6, route 8): 17.84 km, 29 stops, 950 passengers/h → C = 170.1 min, h = 4.29 min, N = 40 buses.

Deliverable: Cycle time, fleet and operated headway

Task 4. Three service alternatives

Repeat the calculation for (A) today's routes with more frequency, (B) a restructured trunk on the shared section with feeders, and (C) the same routes with bus lanes and junction priority. For B, add the transfer: count how many passengers must change and add half the feeder headway to their wait.

Deliverable: Comparison table: fleet, headway, expected wait, transfers, capacity in passengers per hour

AlternativeFleetHeadway (min)Expected wait (min)TransfersCapacity (pass/h)
A Frequency only
B Trunk + feeders
C Priority

Task 5. Select and justify

Select one alternative for Stage 2. Justify it against passenger waiting time, resources (fleet), reliability and street-space needs. Name the group that loses (for example drivers losing a lane, passengers who now transfer).

Deliverable: Half-page justification, included in course-project Stage 2

Materials

  • LAB06_bus_routes.gpkg and LAB06_service_design_synthetic.xlsx
  • P5 scenario results
  • Calculator or Excel
  • Stage 1 counts of buses and passengers on your corridor

Assessment

Continuous assessment (LO4): correct and traceable calculations, sensible rounding to whole buses and timetable headways, and a selection justified against both passenger and operator criteria.

Week 6 · Laboratory LAB6 · 2 hours · QGIS, Excel (QGIS 3.40 LTR or newer)

Cycle time, waiting time and passenger capacity with a bus-priority scenario

Objective

Measure Tashkent's bus routes in GIS (length, stops, spacing, overlap, coverage) and use a spreadsheet model to calculate cycle time, fleet, waiting time and passenger capacity, then compare the effect of a bus-priority measure (LO2, LO4).

Data

SYNTHETIC — generated for teaching: LAB06_service_design_synthetic.xlsx. These files imitate realistic Tashkent conditions but are not observations; the README sheet records the method and random seed.

FileDescriptionSource
LAB06_bus_routes.gpkgLayers: bus_routes (125 OSM relations with length, stops, spacing), route_stops (stop members in order), stop_spacing_segments, route_overlap (route numbers per OSM way), bus_stops, metro_stations, districts (12 study districts)© OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL)
LAB06_route_stops_osm.csvStop and platform members of each bus route relation in relation order, with coordinates (WGS 84 lon/lat), extracted from the Geofabrik .pbf© OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL)
LAB06_service_design_synthetic.xlsxSheets: README, Inputs (operating and priority parameters), Routes (OSM lengths and stops), Service_design (60 route numbers: SYNTHETIC peak loads, cycle time, fleet, headway, wait, capacity, priority scenario), Coverage (area within 500 m; Kontur check), Stop_catchment (Pulatova eqs 2.3–2.4)© OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL); Pulatova (2024); Kontur Population (2023); Synthetic teaching data

All GIS layers use EPSG:32642 (WGS 84 / UTM zone 42N). Set the QGIS project CRS to EPSG:32642 before starting.

Steps

Step 1. Set up the project

  1. Project > Properties > CRS: set EPSG:32642 (WGS 84 / UTM zone 42N)
  2. Layer > Add Layer > Add Vector Layer: open LAB06_bus_routes.gpkg and add all layers
  3. Open the attribute table of bus_routes; sort by ref and look at routes 13Т, 17T and 9Т

Check: bus_routes has 125 features; districts has 12; write one sentence on the inconsistent route refs

Step 2. Route length and stops

  1. Open the Field Calculator on bus_routes: new field len_qgis = $length / 1000 (decimal)
  2. View > Statistical Summary on length_km and on n_stops (filter n_stops >= 2)

Check: len_qgis matches length_km to within 0.1 km (QGIS may measure on the ellipsoid); median length 18.6 km; 117 relations have stops; median 34 stops

Step 3. Stop spacing from the stop sequences

  1. Processing Toolbox > Vector creation > Points to path: input route_stops, order field seq, group field rel_osm_id
  2. Processing Toolbox > Vector geometry > Explode lines on the result; add field spacing = $length
  3. Select by Expression spacing > 3000 and inspect those gaps on the map; then compare with stop_spacing_segments

Check: Two gaps over 3 km (missing stop members); median spacing of the rest = 458 m; 14% of segments are under 300 m

Step 4. Overlapping routes

  1. Style route_overlap with Graduated symbology on n_routes (Natural Breaks), line width by class
  2. Select by Expression n_routes >= 10 and read the refs field of the selected ways

Check: Maximum 18 route numbers on one way; ways with 10 or more routes total 5.0 km

Step 5. Walking coverage of stops

  1. Vector geometry > Buffer bus_stops, distance 500 m, Dissolve = yes; then Vector overlay > Clip with districts
  2. Add field area = $area to the clipped layer and divide by the districts' total area
  3. Repeat with metro_stations; compare with the Coverage sheet; read the Kontur column F and cell F16

Check: Share of area within 500 m: bus stops 0.713, metro 0.079; Kontur places 76% of modelled population in three outer districts

Step 6. Trace one route in the spreadsheet

  1. Open LAB06_service_design_synthetic.xlsx; read the README (SYNTHETIC banner) and the Inputs sheet
  2. Service_design: find route 8; click E to M and read each formula
  3. Recalculate route 8 by hand with the P6 formulas

Check: Route 8: cycle time 170.1 min, fleet 40 buses, operated headway 4.25 min, capacity offered 1,129 places per hour

Step 7. Compare the priority measure

  1. Read columns N–R for route 8 and the totals below the table
  2. Make a clustered column chart (Insert > Chart) of total fleet and mean wait, baseline vs priority

Check: Route 8 priority cycle 142.1 min; total fleet 1,549 → 1,294 buses; mean wait 5.82 → 4.01 min with today's fleet

Step 8. Sensitivity of the priority result

  1. On Inputs, change the bus-lane share (B9) to 0.25 and 0.75; record total fleet and mean wait each time
  2. Return it to 0.5; change the mixed-traffic running speed (B5) from 18 to 15 km/h and record the baseline fleet

Check: A table of four runs with one sentence: which assumption changes the fleet most?

Step 9. Your corridor route

  1. Copy the route 8 row to a new row; enter your corridor route's length and stops (from bus_routes) and your P5 peak load
  2. Stop_catchment sheet: enter your observed mean headway and compare the catchment with the SNiP 500 m norm

Check: Fleet and headway for your route in both scenarios, ready for P6 and Stage 2
