Week 5 · Practical P5 · 2 hours

Trip-demand and mode-share scenarios for your corridor

Objective

Develop trip-demand and mode-share scenarios for the baseline and two alternatives of your project corridor, and explain every assumption (LO2, LO5, LO6).

Tasks

Task 1. Fix the scenario frame

Choose one assessment (horizon) year for all scenarios — the course default is 2035. Define the baseline (trend, committed projects only), a low-cost alternative and a structural alternative. Use the same names as your P4 network alternatives so Stage 2 stays consistent.

Deliverable: Scenario definition table (one row per scenario, measures listed)

Task 2. Build the assumption register

List every input you need: base-year person flow on the corridor (from your Stage 1 counts or LAB2), growth rate, mode shares, car occupancy, trip length. For each input record value, unit, low and high values, status (MEASURED, SOURCED or ASSUMPTION) and source with page or sheet. Use the LAB5 Inputs sheet as a model.

Deliverable: Assumption register

Task 3. Project the demand

Grow the base-year peak-hour person flow to the horizon year: flow × (1 + growth)^years. Keep total person demand the same in all three scenarios unless one of them changes land use — if so, say why.

Deliverable: Horizon-year person demand per scenario

Task 4. Set mode shares with a mechanism

For each scenario, set car, bus/metro and walking/cycling shares. For every change from the baseline, name the mechanism (time, price or convenience) and the measure that causes it. If an alternative adds road capacity, add induced traffic using an elasticity between 0.5 and 1 and explain your choice.

Deliverable: Mode-share table with one-line mechanism per change

Task 5. Results and the weakest assumption

Convert shares into peak-hour car vehicles (person trips ÷ occupancy) and bus passengers per direction — these feed P6 and LAB6. Name the assumption you trust least and say how the result would change at its low and high values.

Deliverable: Results table and a half-page note, carried into course-project Stage 2

Materials

  • Stage 1 corridor counts or LAB2 data
  • LAB05_demand_scenarios_synthetic.xlsx as a template
  • P4 network alternatives
  • Calculator or Excel

Assessment

Continuous assessment (LO2, LO6): every number has a status and source, mode-share changes are explained by a mechanism, and the weakest assumption is identified and tested.

Week 5 · Laboratory LAB5 · 2 hours · Excel (QGIS not used in this lab)

Demand scenarios and sensitivity in a spreadsheet

Objective

Use a spreadsheet model to compare a baseline and two alternatives for Tashkent in 2035, test how growth rates and mode shares change the results, quantify induced demand and critically re-run a published benefit claim (LO2, LO6).

Data

SYNTHETIC — generated for teaching: LAB05_demand_scenarios_synthetic.xlsx. These files imitate realistic Tashkent conditions but are not observations; the README sheet records the method and random seed.

FileDescriptionSource
LAB05_demand_scenarios_synthetic.xlsxSheets: README, Inputs (18 parameters with status and source), Scenarios, Sensitivity (with tornado chart), Growth_x_share, Induced_demand, Claim_check (Pulatova 2024), Cycling (Normukhammadov 2026 Table 4.6), Distribution (synthetic four-zone gravity example). Mixes sourced inputs with labelled teaching assumptions.Normukhammadov (2026); Pulatova (2024); Duranton & Turner (2011); Synthetic teaching data

All GIS layers use EPSG:32642 (WGS 84 / UTM zone 42N). Set the QGIS project CRS to EPSG:32642 before starting.

Steps

Step 1. Read the inputs before the outputs

  1. Open LAB05_demand_scenarios_synthetic.xlsx; read the README sheet, including the SYNTHETIC banner
  2. Inputs sheet: filter column F (Data > Filter) to show only rows whose status starts with ASSUMPTION
  3. For each assumption, write in column H what data would replace it

Check: 18 input rows; 7 of them have the status ASSUMPTION (synthetic)

Step 2. Trace the baseline

  1. Scenarios sheet: click B3, B4 and B5 and read each formula in the formula bar (Formulas > Show Formulas helps)
  2. Write the baseline car-share formula in words: observed share grown with the fleet at the assumed elasticity

Check: Population 2035 = 3,580,965; motorised trips = 7,161,930 per day; baseline car share = 0.868

Step 3. Compare the scenarios

  1. Read row 9 (car vehicle-km per day) and row 10 (change against the baseline) for all three scenarios
  2. Read row 11: public-transport trips divided by the 2.7 million concept target
  3. Insert > Chart > Clustered Column on A9:D9 to chart vehicle-km by scenario

Check: Vehicle-km per day: baseline 38,250,294; A 30,851,389; B 25,121,845 (−34.3%); B carries 1.14 × the target

Step 4. Calibration check against the concept target

  1. Scenarios rows 15–18: compare the trips implied by the concept target with the workbook's base-year trips
  2. Change the trip rate on the Inputs sheet (B7) until the ratio in B18 is 1.00, then put it back to 2.0

Check: Implied trips 6,279,070 per day; ratio 0.97 at the default trip rate

Step 5. One-at-a-time sensitivity and the tornado chart

  1. Sensitivity sheet: check that row 2 equals Scenarios!B9
  2. Data > Sort rows 3–8 by column F (largest to smallest)
  3. Select A1:A8 and G1:H8; Insert > Chart > Bar > Clustered Bar; Format Data Series > Series Overlap 100% to make a tornado

Check: Largest swing: Mean car trip length, 19,125,147 veh-km/day; the sourced fleet growth rate has the smallest swing

Step 6. Growth rate × mode share

  1. Growth_x_share sheet: Home > Conditional Formatting > Color Scales on B3:G6
  2. Find every cell that is at least 30% below the central baseline (38,250,294)

Check: Write which car shares meet a −30% objective at every growth rate tested

Step 7. Induced demand

  1. Induced_demand sheet: read the formula in B5
  2. On Inputs, set the elasticity (B16) to 0.5 and note B6; then return it to 1.0

Check: Extra traffic = 3,825,029 veh-km/day at elasticity 1.0; about half that at 0.5

Step 8. Re-run a published claim

  1. Claim_check sheet: confirm that B7 reproduces the published $19,007,010 per year
  2. Replace the defaults in B10, B11 and B15 with values you can justify, and record the justification in column C

Check: Published value 19,007,010; corrected value with the default assumptions 6,008,538 (ratio 0.32)

Step 9. Cycling scenario and the four-zone distribution

  1. Cycling sheet: compare row 8 (users as share of population) with the Scenarios sheet row 13
  2. Distribution sheet: change β in C1 from 0.15 to 0.10 and 0.25 and record the mean trip length in B15

Check: Central cycling scenario = 15,000,000 km per year; mean trip length at β = 0.15 is 4.22 km

Submission checklist

  • Completed workbook with your assumption notes in Inputs column H and your corrected Claim_check values
  • Tornado chart and a one-paragraph interpretation: which data would you collect first?
  • Table of the three scenarios (vehicle-km, PT trips, change vs baseline) with a sentence on robustness
  • Induced-demand result at elasticity 0.5 and 1.0
  • Half a page of limitations: assumptions without Tashkent sources, motorised-only split, no land-use feedback
  • Input data, method, results and limitations are all included (syllabus §6).
