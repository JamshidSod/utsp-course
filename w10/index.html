Week 10 · Practical P10 · 2 hours

Capacity, queues and delay at your corridor junction

Objective

Calculate capacity, queues and delay for the baseline and for proposed junction-control alternatives at one signalised junction or crossing of your project corridor, reporting vehicle, passenger and pedestrian delay separately (LO4, LO6).

Tasks

Task 1. Describe the junction

Choose the busiest signalised junction (or a crossing you plan to signalise) on your corridor. Draw a plan sketch with approaches, lanes per approach, crosswalks, stops and the phase sequence. Use OSM (LAB10_junction.gpkg or tashkent_base.gpkg) and your Week 7 cross-sections; measure crossing lengths kerb to kerb in QGIS (View > Measure > Measure Line).

Deliverable: Annotated junction sketch with lanes, phases and crossing lengths

Task 2. Collect or estimate the inputs

From your own field count (or the LAB10 values if you could not count), record for each approach: cars and buses per hour, estimated bus occupancy, cycle and green times from at least five stopwatch cycles, queue length at the end of red, and pedestrians per hour on each crosswalk. Measure saturation headways for one lane, as in a published course assignment: ignore the first four vehicles. Mark every value as observed or assumed.

Deliverable: Input table with observed/assumed flag for every value

Task 3. Baseline capacity and degree of saturation

For each lane group compute flow in pcu/h (bus = 2 pcu), saturation flow (lanes × s), capacity c = s × g / C and degree of saturation x = q / c. Flag any lane group with x above 0.85.

Deliverable: Baseline capacity table

Task 4. Baseline queues and delay

Compute Webster average delay for each lane group and the queue at the end of red (q × red time per lane). Compare the predicted queue with your observed queue; if they differ by more than a few vehicles, adjust saturation flow and explain.

Deliverable: Delay and queue table with a model-versus-observation comparison

Task 5. Two control alternatives

Alternative A: operational change only (retiming with Webster's cycle and pedestrian minimum greens, or a turn ban). Alternative B: a geometric or priority change (bus lane, median refuge, queue jump, or active bus priority). Recalculate cycle, greens, x, delay and queues for both. Use the LAB10 workbook structure if you wish — copy a sheet and change the inputs.

Deliverable: Calculation sheets for alternatives A and B

Task 6. Compare and decide

Fill the comparison table below for the baseline and both alternatives. Write five sentences: who gains, who loses, which result depends most on an assumption, and which alternative you carry into Stage 3 of the course project.

Week 10 · Laboratory LAB10 · 2 hours · QGIS, Excel (QGIS 3.40 LTR or newer)

Comparing junction-control alternatives

Objective

Compare a baseline signal timing with three junction-control alternatives at a real Tashkent junction using a spreadsheet model, report vehicle delay and passenger delay separately, test sensitivity, and map the city's signal inventory in QGIS (LO4, LO6).

Data

SYNTHETIC — generated for teaching: LAB10_synthetic_junction.xlsx. These files imitate realistic Tashkent conditions but are not observations; the README sheet records the method and random seed.

FileDescriptionSource
LAB10_junction.gpkgLayers: districts (12 study districts), traffic_signals (1,867 OSM nodes), major_roads, roads_site_500m, junction_site (Shahrisabz St × Istiqbol St), junction_approaches (M1, M2, S1, S2 with compass labels and the synthetic flows)© OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL)
LAB10_synthetic_junction.xlsxSYNTHETIC flows, occupancies, pedestrian flows and existing timing (Inputs sheet, yellow cells); sheets Baseline, Alt1_Retimed, Alt2_BusLane, Alt3_BusLane_Refuge with live Webster formulas; Summary with chartSynthetic teaching data; Webster (1958)

All GIS layers use EPSG:32642 (WGS 84 / UTM zone 42N). Set the QGIS project CRS to EPSG:32642 before starting.

Steps

Step 1. Set up the project

  1. Project > Properties > CRS: set EPSG:32642 (WGS 84 / UTM zone 42N)
  2. Layer > Add Layer > Add Vector Layer: open LAB10_junction.gpkg and add all layers
  3. Style traffic_signals as small red dots and districts with no fill

Check: 12 district polygons; traffic_signals has 1,867 features

Step 2. Signal inventory by district

  1. Processing Toolbox > Vector analysis > Count points in polygon: polygons = districts, points = traffic_signals, count field NUMPOINTS
  2. Open the attribute table, Field Calculator: new field per_km2 = "NUMPOINTS" / "area_km2" (decimal)
  3. Symbology > Graduated on per_km2; sort the attribute table by per_km2

Check: Total NUMPOINTS = 1,799; Mirobod is densest (≈10.7 per km²) and Bektemir sparsest (≈0.67 per km²)

Step 3. Nodes are not junctions

  1. Vector geometry > Buffer: input traffic_signals, distance 30 m, Dissolve result = yes
  2. Vector geometry > Multipart to singleparts on the buffer output
  3. Count the features; zoom to three clusters and check that each is one junction

Check: 729 clusters; write one sentence on why 1,867 nodes overstate the number of signalised junctions

Step 4. The study junction

  1. Right-click junction_site > Zoom to Layer; label junction_approaches with approach and compass
  2. View > Measure > Measure Line: measure kerb-to-kerb crossing length of Shahrisabz St and of Istiqbol St on a satellite basemap (XYZ Tiles) or the OSM road widths
  3. Select by Location: traffic_signals within 500 m of junction_site (buffer it first with Vector geometry > Buffer, 500 m)

Check: 24 signal nodes within 500 m; your crossing lengths compared with the workbook values (24 m and 14 m) — note the difference

Step 5. Read the baseline model

  1. Open LAB10_synthetic_junction.xlsx; read the README (SYNTHETIC data, method, formulas)
  2. On the Baseline sheet click M2 and use Formulas > Trace Precedents to follow the Webster delay formula
  3. Check columns G–L by hand for approach M1: flow, saturation flow, y, g, λ and x

Check: Baseline cycle 120 s; average vehicle delay 19.2 s/veh; maximum degree of saturation 0.62

Step 6. Retime the junction (Alt 1)

  1. On Alt1_Retimed, read the timing block below the lane groups: critical y, Y, L, Webster C0, pedestrian minimum greens
  2. Explain in two sentences why the cycle used (80 s) is twice Webster's C0

Check: C0 = 39.5 s; cycle needed for pedestrians = 78.6 s; cycle used 80 s; vehicle delay 14.3 s/veh

Step 7. Vehicle delay and passenger delay separately

  1. Go to Summary and read the chart: per vehicle, per occupant, per pedestrian
  2. Rank the four alternatives twice: by average vehicle delay and by average occupant delay
  3. Copy the Summary table into your report and write which ranking you would show the city, and why

Check: Vehicle delay 19.2 / 14.3 / 18.9 / 15.1 s; occupant delay 18.4 / 13.7 / 15.0 / 11.8 s; Alt 1 ranks first by vehicles, Alt 3 by passengers

Step 8. Sensitivity to bus occupancy (Goal Seek)

  1. In Summary!G4 type =D4-C4 (Alt 2 minus Alt 1 occupant delay)
  2. Data > What-If Analysis > Goal Seek: Set cell G4, To value 0, By changing Inputs!B5 (bus occupancy)
  3. Record the break-even occupancy; repeat with =E4-C4 for Alt 3; restore Inputs!B5 to 55

Check: Break-even ≈ 120.2 passengers per bus for Alt 2 and ≈ 10.8 for Alt 3

Step 9. Sensitivity to walking speed

  1. Set Inputs!B8 (pedestrian crossing speed) to 1.0 m/s for older pedestrians; read Alt1_Retimed again
  2. Restore 1.2 m/s afterwards

Check: Alt 1 cycle rises to 90 s and vehicle delay to 15.5 s/veh

Submission checklist

  • District signal-density map (PNG or PDF, with legend, source and date)
  • Cluster count with a one-sentence explanation of nodes versus junctions
  • Summary table and chart from the workbook, with vehicle, passenger and pedestrian delay in separate columns
  • Break-even occupancies and the walking-speed test, with two sentences on robustness
  • Half a page of limitations: synthetic flows, Webster assumptions, two-phase simplification, crossing lengths
  • Input data, method, results and limitations are all included (syllabus §6).

  • TU Delft (2014). CIE4821 Traffic Flow Theory and Simulation. TU Delft OpenCourseWare. — Bottlenecks and shockwaves lectures; past exams with answers. https://ocw.tudelft.nl/courses/traffic-flow-theory-simulation/
