Week 11 · Practical P11 · 2 hours

A parking and loading policy for your corridor

Objective

Develop a parking and loading policy for your project corridor, explain its impacts on each stakeholder group and set out the implementation arrangements (LO3, LO5).

Tasks

Task 1. Kerb inventory

On a base map of your corridor (OSM roads and LAB11-style kerb segments drawn in QGIS), mark legal parking, no-stopping zones, bus stops, taxi stands, loading places and driveways. Count legal spaces as kerb length ÷ 6 m. Mark where cars park on footways.

Deliverable: Kerb inventory map with legal spaces per segment

Task 2. Quick occupancy and delivery observation

Patrol two kerb segments at least four times (for example 09:00, 11:00, 14:00, 18:00) and record vehicle codes (not plates), class and position. Log every delivery van for one hour: arrival, departure, position. If you cannot survey safely, use the LAB11 data and say so.

Deliverable: Observation sheet and a short table of occupancy, footway parking and deliveries

Task 3. Diagnose

Write five sentences: when is the kerb full, who occupies it longest, where do deliveries stop, which uses conflict with walking, cycling or buses, and what you do not know.

Deliverable: Diagnosis paragraph

Task 4. Policy package

Propose a kerb plan by time of day (use the illustrative plan from LEC21 as a model) and a package of at most five measures: pricing or time limits, loading bays and hours, taxi bays, footway protection, enforcement. For loading bays, size them with: bays = busiest-hour deliveries × mean dwell (min) ÷ 60 ÷ 0.7.

Deliverable: Kerb plan drawing and list of measures with bay sizing

Task 5. Stakeholder impacts and implementation

Fill the table below for each measure. Name the responsible body, who gains, who loses, what must be agreed and how you will know whether it works (one indicator).

Week 11 · Laboratory LAB11 · 2 hours · QGIS, Excel (QGIS 3.40 LTR or newer)

Kerbside occupancy, turnover and loading scenarios

Objective

Analyse an occupancy and turnover survey of four kerb segments, size a loading area, compare time-limit, pricing and loading-window scenarios, and test an OSM-based capacity proxy for off-street parking (LO3, LO5).

Data

SYNTHETIC — generated for teaching: LAB11_synthetic_kerbside_survey.xlsx. These files imitate realistic Tashkent conditions but are not observations; the README sheet records the method and random seed.

FileDescriptionSource
LAB11_parking.gpkgLayers: districts (12 study districts), parking_polygons (1,959 OSM car parks with parking, fee, capacity, area_m2, district), parking_nodes, market (Mirobod bozori), roads_site_300m, kerb_segments (A1, A2, B1, B2 with legal_spaces), bus_stops_site© OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL)
LAB11_synthetic_kerbside_survey.xlsxSYNTHETIC beat survey (Observations, 30-min rounds 08:00–20:00) and continuous Delivery_log; sheets Inputs (yellow assumptions), Segments, Durations, Occupancy (with chart), Summary, Loading, Scenarios; OSM_calibration and OSM_districts for the capacity proxySynthetic teaching data; © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL); Normukhammadov (2026)

All GIS layers use EPSG:32642 (WGS 84 / UTM zone 42N). Set the QGIS project CRS to EPSG:32642 before starting.

Steps

Step 1. Set up the project

  1. Project > Properties > CRS: set EPSG:32642 (WGS 84 / UTM zone 42N)
  2. Layer > Add Layer > Add Vector Layer: open LAB11_parking.gpkg and add all layers
  3. Label kerb_segments with segment and legal_spaces; zoom to the market layer

Check: 1,959 parking polygons; four kerb segments with 11, 14, 14 and 14 legal spaces (53 in total)

Step 2. Calibrate the capacity proxy

  1. Select by Expression on parking_polygons: "capacity" > 0
  2. Field Calculator (selected features only): new field m2_space = "area_m2" / "capacity"
  3. Vector analysis > Basic statistics for fields: input parking_polygons (selected features only), field m2_space; read the median
  4. Compare with OSM_calibration!B2 in the workbook

Check: 25 tagged car parks; median ≈ 22.9 m² per space; capacity proxy for the city ≈ 148,000 spaces (≈ 48.7 per 1,000 residents)

Step 3. Map capacity by district

  1. Vector analysis > Statistics by categories: input parking_polygons, field to calculate statistics on = area_m2, field with categories = district
  2. Districts > Properties > Joins > +: join layer = the statistics output, join field = district, target field = name_en, custom prefix stats_
  3. Field Calculator on districts: new decimal field spaces_km2 = "stats_sum" / 22.9 / "area_km2"
  4. Graduated symbology on spaces_km2; compare with the OSM_districts sheet

Check: Chilonzor has the highest proxy density; your values match OSM_districts column F within 1%

Step 4. Read the survey data

  1. Open the workbook README: SYNTHETIC data, beat interval, seed, no personal data
  2. On Observations, filter column E (Position) to footway and double-parked
  3. On Durations, check one vehicle by hand: first seen, last seen, duration = last − first + 0.5 h

Check: 844 observations of 267 vehicles; 7% of observations are on the footway

Step 5. Occupancy and turnover

  1. Occupancy sheet: read the line chart of all kerb vs all parked vehicles
  2. Summary sheet: read peak and average occupancy, turnover and duration per segment
  3. Which segment is fullest, and at what time? Write two sentences

Check: Peak kerb occupancy 89% overall and 100% on A1; average 59%; turnover 5.04 vehicles per space per day; mean stay 1.65 h

Step 6. Who uses the kerb longest

  1. Summary: compare the long-stay share of vehicles (cell H6) with the long-stay share of vehicle-hours (cell B9)
  2. Insert > Chart: a two-bar chart of these shares for your report

Check: Long stays are 10% of vehicles but 51% of vehicle-hours

Step 7. Loading-area layout

  1. Loading sheet: read busiest-hour deliveries, mean dwell and bays needed today and with the 08:00–11:00 window
  2. In QGIS, create a new polygon layer (Layer > Create Layer > New GeoPackage Layer, EPSG:32642) and draw the window bays, 12 m each, on segment A1/A2 near the market entrance, keeping 10 m clear of the bus stop
  3. Measure the kerb they take (View > Measure > Measure Line)

Check: 9 deliveries in the busiest hour, mean dwell 19.3 min; 5 bays today, 6 with the window, using 72 m of kerb (12 spaces)

Step 8. Compare kerb policies

  1. Scenarios sheet: read the peak demand ratio for S0–S3
  2. Change Inputs!B4 (2-hour limit compliance) from 0.7 to 0.4 and record S1; restore 0.7
  3. Change Inputs!B5 and B6 (pricing shares) to test a weaker price response; restore them

Check: Peak demand ratio 1.08 / 0.75 / 0.75 / 0.73; S0 has 4 vehicles off the kerb at the peak, S3 none

Submission checklist

  • District map of off-street parking capacity proxy (with legend, source, date and the m² per space you used)
  • Summary table and occupancy chart for the four segments
  • Loading-bay layout (GeoPackage layer and map) with the sizing calculation
  • Scenario table with your two sensitivity tests and three sentences on what the city should do
  • Half a page of limitations: synthetic data, beat interval, capacity proxy, assumed scenario responses
  • Input data, method, results and limitations are all included (syllabus §6).
