Week 9 · Practical P9 · 2 hours

Prioritising maintenance and adaptation measures on your corridor

Objective

Prioritise maintenance and adaptation measures for your corridor using asset condition, service importance and climate risk, and justify the order of works (LO4, LO5).

Tasks

Task 1. Inventory the corridor's assets

Divide your corridor into sections of 100–200 m. For each section list the assets (carriageway, footways, cycle track, trees, aryks/drains, stops, signals, lighting) and record their condition from LAB9 layers, LAB8 audit photos or a site visit (good / fair / poor).

Deliverable: Asset inventory table by section

SectionAssetCondition (G/F/P)Evidence (layer, photo, visit)Owner (if known)
1
2
3

Task 2. Score importance and risk

For each section give a service-importance class (1–3: users, stops, schools, metro access) and a climate-risk class (1–3: shade, drainage, flooding history, wide sunlit carriageway). Use the LAB9 Rules sheet or justify your own rules.

Deliverable: Scored table: condition × importance × risk

Task 3. Choose measures

For the five highest-scoring sections choose a measure (routine maintenance, resurfacing, tree planting with water supply, aryk restoration, shaded stop, drainage) and estimate a cost class (low / medium / high).

Deliverable: List of five measures with cost classes

Task 4. Phase and protect service

Order the measures into a three-year programme. For each, state how pedestrians, cyclists and buses will be kept moving during works, and what you will measure afterwards (e.g. shade class, surface vibration, ponding after rain).

Deliverable: Three-year phasing table with continuity and monitoring notes (feeds project Stage 3, Week 10, and Stage 4, Week 14)

Materials

  • LAB9 results (LAB09_street_assets.gpkg, LAB09_asset_priority.xlsx)
  • LAB8 audit photos
  • Corridor base map

Assessment

Continuous assessment (LO4, LO5): transparent scoring rules, a priority order that follows from them, realistic measures and a credible plan for service continuity.

Week 9 · Laboratory LAB9 · 2 hours · QGIS, Excel (QGIS 3.40 LTR or newer)

Street assets, vulnerable sections and priority locations for shade and drainage

Objective

Create GIS layers of street assets and heat- and drainage-vulnerable sections, score them by condition × importance × risk, and map priority locations for shade or drainage improvements (LO4, LO5).

All GIS layers use EPSG:32642 (WGS 84 / UTM zone 42N). Set the QGIS project CRS to EPSG:32642 before starting.

Steps

Step 1. Set up and inspect the shade asset layer

  1. Project > Properties > CRS: EPSG:32642; add all layers of LAB09_street_assets.gpkg
  2. Style walk_segments_shade by shade_class (Categorized, 1 = dark red … 5 = dark green)
  3. Processing Toolbox > Vector analysis > Statistics by categories: field = length_m, category = shade_class

Check: 2,896 segments, 325.4 km in total; classes 1–2 (Phase 1) sum to 81.7 km

Step 2. Rebuild the importance flags

  1. Vector > Geoprocessing Tools > Buffer: metro_stations by 400 m
  2. Vector > Research Tools > Select by Location: walk_segments_shade that intersect the buffer; compare with near_metro
  3. Repeat with schools buffered by 300 m and compare with near_school

Check: Your selections match the near_metro and near_school columns (small differences only at buffer edges)

Step 3. Map drainage and heat vulnerability

  1. Buffer aryks_drains by 15 m; Select by Location: segments that do NOT intersect (use 'disjoint'); compare with aryk_15m = 0
  2. Processing Toolbox > Vector geometry > Geometry by expression: input walk_segments_shade, output type Point, expression line_interpolate_point($geometry, length($geometry) / 2) (segment midpoints)
  3. Select main_roads with to_int("lanes") >= 3 OR "highway" = 'trunk' (Select by Expression); then Processing Toolbox > Vector selection > Select within distance: midpoints within 25 m of the selected roads; compare with wide_road
  4. Open a satellite basemap and check five 'no aryk' segments: is an aryk really missing, piped or just unmapped?

Check: 90.5% of network length has no mapped aryk within 15 m; record how many of your five checks were OSM gaps

Step 4. Score condition × importance × risk in Excel

  1. Open LAB09_asset_priority.xlsx, sheet Segments: read the formulas in columns K–P
  2. Sheet Summary: note high-priority segments and length
  3. Sheet Rules: change 'High priority if score ≥' from 18 to 12 and record how the high-priority length changes; then restore it

Check: With the default rules: 356 high-priority segments, 32.5 km; 20.2 km of them within 400 m of a metro station

Step 5. Map priority locations for shade or drainage

  1. Style walk_segments_shade by priority (Categorized: High, Medium, Low) and label High segments by measure
  2. Project > New Print Layout: add map, legend, scale bar, north arrow and a source note
  3. Mark the three high-priority locations nearest your corridor and describe the measure for each

Check: A print-ready map with the three locations marked and their measures listed

Step 6. Cycle-path condition and climate indicators

  1. Style cycle_path_condition by condition; Statistics by categories on length_m
  2. Sheet Climate_by_year: check the COUNTIFS formula for days ≥ 40 °C and compare the decade means
  3. Insert > Chart > Column for days ≥ 40 °C by year

Check: Poor-surface cycle path = 28.6 km; days ≥ 40 °C average 0.5 (1991–2000) and 8.3 (2016–2025)

Submission checklist

  • Priority map (print layout) with legend, sources and your three marked locations
  • Completed workbook with one alternative rule set and a note on how the ranking changed
  • Table of five checked 'no aryk' segments (real gap, piped, or unmapped)
  • Chart of days ≥ 40 °C by year with a two-sentence interpretation
  • Half a page of limitations: OSM aryk and tree gaps, survey covers only the centre, reanalysis vs station data, subjective classes
  • Input data, method, results and limitations are all included (syllabus §6).
